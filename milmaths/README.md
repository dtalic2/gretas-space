# 🧮 MilMATHS

Four stages of maths, ten lessons and a test each, and one milbil who sits
beside you the whole way through. Every stage teaches a harder slice of the
**Australian Curriculum** than the one before, every question you answer earns
**XP**, and XP buys milbils in the shop — or the parts to build one of your own.

**▶ Play it: https://dtalic2.github.io/gretas-space/milmaths/**

No build step and no dependencies. Run it locally with:

```bash
python3 milmaths/serve.py      # http://localhost:8134
```

## Three tabs

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🏠 | **Home** | Your stage: the lesson you are on, the comics in front of it, the test at the end, and the time scale for today. |
| 📚 | **Library** | All sixteen comics. Anything you have unlocked can be read again whenever you like. |
| 👾 | **Milbils** | The shop, the workshop where you build your own, and the features. |

## The four stages

You start at Stage 1, lesson 1, and nothing else in the game is reachable.

| | Stage | Australian Curriculum | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🔢 | **1 · Numbers and Place Value** | Year 3 · Number | Place value, adding and subtracting, rounding, patterns, Australian money |
| ✖️ | **2 · Times, Shares and Fractions** | Year 4 · Number | Times tables, dividing, remainders, factors and multiples, fractions |
| 📐 | **3 · Decimals, Measuring and Shape** | Year 5 · Number, Measurement & Space | Decimals, percentages, metric units, perimeter and area, angles, time |
| 🧮 | **4 · Algebra, Integers and Chance** | Year 6–7 · Algebra, Number & Probability | Order of operations, negative numbers, powers, algebra, equations, ratio, probability, averages |

**40 lessons and 4 tests — 348 questions.** Every lesson names the curriculum
code it comes from, so a grown-up can see exactly where it sits.

## One lesson at a time

- Each lesson has between **4 and 10 questions**.
- The lesson you are on is the green one. Everything below it is locked.
- Every question gives you **two goes**. Get it wrong twice and MilMATHS shows
  you the answer and explains it, which still earns a little.
- You can go back over anything you have already done — but **a lesson only pays
  XP the first time**. Going back is for the stars, not the money.

### Stars

Stars count the questions you did **not** get right first go.

| Stars | Wrong | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★★★ | none | everything right first time |
| ★★ | one | |
| ★ | two | |
| ☆☆☆ | **three or more** | **the lesson does not count** |

No stars means the lesson stays open and so does everything behind it. Read the
explanations, go again, and two wrong will do it. Nothing else is lost — the XP
you earned on the way through is yours either way.

## The stage test

At the end of every stage there is a **test of fifteen questions**, on exactly
the material the stage taught but asked harder: bigger numbers, two steps
instead of one, a fact turned round. It is not a lesson:

- **One go** at each question.
- **Scratch pad only** — no hints, no number line, no times table poster.
- **More than three wrong and the whole stage starts again.** All ten lessons
  unlock and have to be worked through from lesson 1.

Failing costs you lessons and nothing else. Your XP, your comics, your milbils
and everything you have bought stay exactly where they are, and the lessons you
redo simply pay no XP the second time.

**Passing the test is the only thing that opens the next stage.**

## The comics

Sixteen of them, one in front of lessons **1, 4, 7 and 10** of every stage. Each
is a two- or three-page comic in which milbils you have met in the other games
work the idea out loud before you are asked to use it — Chomp counting the
biscuit jar for place value, Giggle trying to take the biggest slice to learn
that 1/8 is smaller than 1/4, Chomp and Wizzle falling out over a café bill to
learn BIDMAS.

The first time you open one is worth 15 XP, the comic in front of a lesson opens
by itself the first time you start that lesson, and every one of them lives in
the **Library** tab afterwards.

The pictures are drawn in SVG against the theme's own colours, so they turn into
chalk on the Blackboard theme without a second copy of anything.

A clean run of MilMATHS — every lesson, every comic, every test, nothing wrong —
pays about **6,900 XP**, and there is a great deal more than that on the

Spending never costs you a rank. MilMATHS keeps two numbers: the XP in your
pocket, which goes down when you buy something, and every XP you have *ever*
earned, which only ever goes up. Ranks — Counter, Adder, Timeser, Fractioneer,
Decimalist, Measurer, Algebraist, Milbil Maths Champion — run off the second.

## Two different clocks

They measure different things, and the game keeps them apart on purpose.

**The countdown (⏱ at the top)** is how long you are here for today. You set it,
it counts down, and it runs **wherever you are in the game — the shop
included** — because it is measuring the time you have been sitting at the
screen. It stops when you switch away from the tab, and it resets at midnight.
**It never stops you doing anything.**

**The 0–300 minute scale (on Home)** is how long you have actually spent on
maths. It only counts time in a **lesson, a test or a comic**. Browsing the
shop, building a milbil and reading the settings do not move it at all. The
little gold mark on the scale is today's suggestion.

### What it suggests, and why

MilMATHS works out a number from three things: **what day it is**, **what time
it is** and **which public holidays are on where you live**.

The day sets the starting figure:

| Day | Suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Christmas Day, Good Friday, New Year's Day, Anzac Day, your birthday | **nothing at all** |
| Christmas Eve | 5 minutes |
| Any other public holiday in your state | 10 minutes |
| School holidays, weekday | 20 minutes |
| Saturday or Sunday | 15 minutes |
| Friday | 10 minutes — you have done a whole week of school |
| Monday to Thursday in term time | 15 minutes |
| Pi Day (14 March) or a leap day | 5 minutes extra, for luck |

The time of day can only ever take minutes *away*:

| Time | Caps it at |
| --- | --- |
| Before 6 am | 5 minutes — go back to bed |
| 6–8:30 am on a school day | 10 minutes — a warm-up, not a lesson |
| 9 am–3 pm on a school day | 5 minutes — you should be at school! |
| 7–8:30 pm | 10 minutes |
| After 8:30 pm | 5 minutes |

### Public holidays

Worked out in the browser, with no website involved, for whichever state you
pick in Settings: New Year's Day, Australia Day, Good Friday, Easter Saturday,
Easter Sunday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday, Labour Day (which
is in March, May or October depending on where you live), Christmas Day and
Boxing Day — plus the ones only one state gets: Canberra Day, Adelaide Cup Day,
Western Australia Day, Picnic Day, Melbourne Cup Day, Grand Final Friday, the
Royal Hobart Regatta and the Royal Queensland Show.

Weekend holidays shift properly. Christmas on a Saturday gives you the Monday
*and* the Tuesday, which is the bit most calendars get wrong. Easter is computed
with the anonymous Gregorian algorithm, so it is right in every year rather than
looked up in a table that runs out.

**Two things are deliberately approximate**, and the game says so on the Today
screen: school term dates (they differ by state and move every year, so the four
breaks are averaged) and the handful of holidays proclaimed year by year
(Western Australia's King's Birthday, Brisbane's show day).

### Coming back after a while

If you have not opened MilMATHS for **two days or more**, the next time you do
it says so before anything else:

> ⚠️ **Caution** — You haven't played this game for a long time. We strongly
> recommend that you keep reinforcing your learning.

Nothing is lost when that happens. Your stage, your stars and your XP are
exactly where you left them.

## Your name

You pick it on the way in, and you can change it later in Settings along with
your state, your birthday, the theme and the sound. Your birthday is optional —
MilMATHS just gives you the day off if you tell it.

## How it is built

Plain ES modules, no framework, no build step, nothing loaded from anywhere
else. Everything — fonts, pictures, the holiday calendar — is in this folder.

```
milmaths/
├── index.html
├── manifest.json        installable, works offline once loaded
├── icon.svg
├── serve.py             local server with no-cache headers
├── art/                 Greta's drawings: Chomp, Petal, Sunny, Hattie, Scoop
├── css/style.css        three themes out of one set of variables
└── js/
    ├── curriculum.js    4 stages, 40 lessons, 4 tests, 348 questions
    ├── books.js         the 16 comics, their cast and their pictures
    ├── shop.js          18 milbils, 39 workshop parts, 12 features, 8 ranks
    ├── clock.js         holidays, recommendations and the countdown
    ├── state.js         everything saved, and all the locking rules
    ├── lesson.js        running a lesson or a test: marking, XP, stars
    ├── milbil.js        drawing milbils (shared with Milbil Café)
    ├── ui.js            sheets, toasts, cards, confetti, sound
    └── main.js          the three tabs and every screen
```

Everything is saved to `localStorage` under `milmaths-v1`. Settings → **Start
MilMATHS again** wipes it, keeping only your name and your state.

### Checking the maths

The question bank is plain data, so it can be checked by machine. 113 of the 348
questions are bare arithmetic, and those are re-computed from the question text
and compared with the stored answer; the structure of all 348 is checked at the
same time — choices in range, no duplicate options, 4–10 questions a lesson and
10–20 a test, every question explained. The comics are checked the same way:
every character named in a panel exists, every picture referenced is drawn, and
there is exactly one comic in front of lessons 1, 4, 7 and 10 of every stage.

The game has also been played end to end in a real browser — all 40 lessons and
all 4 tests, in order, answering every question correctly — to confirm that the
stages unlock in the right order and that nothing can be reached early. The
awkward paths were driven too: three wrong in a lesson, four wrong in a test, a
reload in the middle, and a save two days old.
